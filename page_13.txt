Section 10  |  Innovation
Smart Share's contribution is not simply splitting a cab fare. It is deciding which strangers can practically
share a journey, and showing why. It combines several checks that simple proximity matching lacks:
  Location-aware pickup discovery using spatial indexing.
 Time compatibility through shared departure-window overlap.
 Direction and destination compatibility, so a rider heading north is never paired with one heading south.
 Multiple valid pickup/drop-off sequences, with riders required to share the cab for part of the trip.
 Detour-aware feasibility: each rider's extra time is measured against their own limit.
 Capacity constraints as a hard gate.
 Ranked, explainable results, with honest labels when routing is approximate.

 Economic: sharing a cab could reduce what each rider pays, and fills seats that would otherwise travel empty.
 Environmental: where pooling replaces separate trips, fewer vehicle trips could mean less fuel use and fewer
emissions.
 Social: easier discovery of fellow travellers and simpler coordination, potentially making transport more
accessible.
 Operational: better use of existing vehicle capacity.
None of these benefits has been measured. Realising them depends on user adoption, on fare logic still to be
built, and on matches converting into real shared trips, which the current version does not handle.
